Long-Acting Reversible Contraception

Gold standard for reversible contraception. User-independent, highly effective, and safe for most populations including nulliparous women.

⚡ Copper IUD Non-Hormonal

  • Mechanism: Cu²⁺ ions → spermicidal inflammatory reaction in endometrium. Primarily prevents fertilization.
  • Duration: 10–12 years.
  • Advantage: Hormone-free; ideal for women who cannot use hormones. Also the most effective emergency contraceptive.
  • Downside: Worsens menorrhagia and dysmenorrhea — avoid in women with heavy/painful periods.
  • Special: Avoid in Wilson's Disease (copper accumulation risk).

🩸 Levonorgestrel IUS (Mirena) Progestin

  • Mechanism: Thins endometrium + thickens cervical mucus → reduces sperm penetration.
  • Duration: 3–8 years (device-dependent).
  • Advantage: Reduces menstrual blood loss (amenorrhoea in ~50% by year 2). Treats endometriosis pain and menorrhagia.
  • Downside: Irregular/unpredictable spotting in first 3–6 months; counsel patients proactively.
  • Choice for: Menorrhagia, dysmenorrhoea, endometriosis, anaemia from heavy periods.

💪 Subdermal Implant (Nexplanon)

  • Mechanism: Sustained-release etonogestrel → inhibits ovulation + thickens cervical mucus.
  • Duration: 3 years.
  • Efficacy: 0.05% — the single most effective reversible method.
  • Placement: Subdermally in non-dominant upper arm. Requires trained inserter.
  • Main side effect: Unpredictable, irregular bleeding patterns. Counsel before insertion.
🏆 Highest Efficacy of All Reversible Methods 0.05% failure rate. If a patient needs maximum protection and doesn't want a uterine device, this is the answer.

Hormonal & Barrier Methods

💊 Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill (COCP)

  • Active Agents: Estrogen (Ethinylestradiol) + Progestin.
  • Primary Mechanism: Reliably suppresses ovulation via negative feedback on HPO axis.
  • Non-contraceptive Benefits:
    • Treats acne & hirsutism (especially Diane-35 / Cyproterone Acetate — raises SHBG, lowers free testosterone)
    • Regulates cycle; treats PMDD, endometriosis, dysmenorrhoea
    • Reduces risk of ovarian and endometrial cancer
  • Myth Busted: NOT associated with clinically significant weight gain.
  • Rapid fertility return after stopping — preferred in newly married/nulliparous women.

💉 DMPA Injection (Depo-Provera)

  • Frequency: IM or SC injection every 12 weeks (3 months).
  • Side Effects (proven):
    • Weight gain (actual metabolic effect)
    • Reversible bone density loss with prolonged use — counsel about calcium/Vitamin D
    • Delayed return to fertility: 9–12 months (sometimes up to 2 years)
  • Ideal for:
    • Epilepsy — raises seizure threshold; compatible with anti-epileptics
    • Sickle Cell Disease — reduces sickling crises
  • Avoid if patient plans pregnancy soon — delayed fertility return.

🌙 Progestin-Only Pill (POP / Mini-Pill)

  • Mechanism: Thickens cervical mucus (primary), may inhibit ovulation at higher doses.
  • Key use: Breastfeeding mothers <6 months postpartum — does not suppress lactation.
  • Also safe in: Migraine with aura, DVT/PE history, smokers >35.
  • Caution: Must be taken at same time daily (3-hour window for traditional POP).
  • Bariatric surgery: Consider alternative — malabsorption may reduce efficacy.

🛡️ Barrier Methods

  • Male Condom: The only method that also protects against STIs and HIV. Always counsel dual protection.
  • Female Condom: Polyurethane; also STI protective. Can be inserted hours before intercourse.
  • Diaphragm: Must be used with spermicide. Leave in situ for ≥6 hours post-coitus. Risk: recurrent UTI, TSS (rare).
  • Cervical Cap: Less effective in parous women.

WHO Medical Eligibility — Absolute Contraindications

Category 4 conditions represent unacceptable health risks where the method must not be used. Memorise these — they are heavily tested.

Estrogen-Containing Methods

COCP Patch Vaginal Ring
  • Migraine WITH Aura — Stroke risk (independent risk factor).
  • History of DVT / PE or known thrombogenic mutation (Factor V Leiden, etc.).
  • Uncontrolled Hypertension ≥160/100 mmHg.
  • Smoker ≥ 35 years (≥15 cigarettes/day).
  • Current or Recent Breast Cancer.
  • Postpartum < 21 days (VTE risk heightened).
  • Breastfeeding < 6 weeks postpartum (suppresses lactation).
  • Liver disease: Severe cirrhosis, hepatic adenoma, acute viral hepatitis.
  • Structural heart disease with risk of atrial fibrillation / valvular disease.

Intrauterine Devices (IUDs)

Copper IUD Mirena IUS
  • Known or suspected pregnancy.
  • Unexplained vaginal bleeding (until malignancy excluded).
  • Active pelvic infection: PID, gonorrhoea/chlamydia cervicitis, puerperal sepsis.
  • Anatomic distortion of uterine cavity (fibroids, congenital anomaly).
  • Gestational trophoblastic disease with persistently elevated βhCG.
  • Wilson's Disease (Copper IUD specifically) — risk of copper accumulation.

Progestin-Only Methods

DMPA POP Implant
  • Current Breast Cancer (all progestin-containing methods).
  • History of bariatric surgery (malabsorption — for oral POP specifically; injectable/implant unaffected).
  • DMPA-specific: Unexplained vaginal bleeding (until cause identified); known or suspected pregnancy.
Exam Pearl Progestin-only methods are generally SAFE where estrogen is contraindicated — migraine with aura, DVT/PE history, smoker >35, breastfeeding.

Emergency Contraception

Prevents pregnancy after unprotected intercourse. Does not terminate an established pregnancy — ineffective once implantation has occurred. Prescribe within windows below.

Method Window Efficacy BMI Consideration Key Notes
1. Copper IUD ≤ 5 days (120 hrs) 99.9% Not Affected Most effective option. Provides ongoing long-term contraception. Method of choice in high BMI.
2. Ulipristal Acetate (EllaOne / Ella) ≤ 5 days (120 hrs) High Less effective BMI >30 Prescription only. Selective progesterone receptor modulator.
Wait 5 days before starting hormonal contraception (risk of interaction).
3. Levonorgestrel (Plan B / Postinor) ≤ 3 days (72 hrs) Moderate Less effective BMI ≥25 OTC. Works best within 72 hrs, but licensed up to 120 hrs.
Start ongoing hormonal BC immediately (no waiting period).
4. Yuzpe Method ≤ 3–5 days Low — High-dose combined COCPs as EC (last resort). Severe nausea — prescribe antiemetic. Only use when no other option available.
Quick Start Protocol If pregnancy is reasonably excluded, start ongoing contraception immediately — do not wait for the next menstrual period. Use backup method (condoms) for 7 days.
⚠ Ulipristal Interaction Ulipristal and hormonal contraceptives compete at the progesterone receptor. Wait 5 days after taking ulipristal before starting a hormonal method. Use condoms in the interim.

Sterilization (Permanent)

For individuals and couples who have completed family size and desire a permanent, irreversible solution.

♀ Female Sterilization

  • Gold Standard Technique: Salpingectomy (bilateral tube removal) — preferred over tubal ligation/clips.
  • Bonus benefit: Salpingectomy reduces ovarian cancer risk (most ovarian cancers originate in the fimbriae).
  • Immediate efficacy post-procedure.
  • Risks: Surgical complications (anaesthesia, haemorrhage). If rare method failure occurs, ectopic pregnancy risk is increased — patient must know symptoms.

♂ Male Sterilization (Vasectomy)

  • Safer, cheaper, and more effective than female sterilization. Performed under local anaesthesia as an outpatient.
  • Critical NOT immediately effective. Sperm persist in the reproductive tract post-procedure.
  • Backup contraception required for 3 months / 20 ejaculations.
  • Confirmation: Semen analysis to confirm azoospermia before stopping backup method.
  • Should be considered permanent — reversal success rates decline over time.
Exam Trap: Vasectomy is NOT immediate If asked "what backup is needed after vasectomy?" — Answer: condoms for 3 months / 20 ejaculations until azoospermia confirmed on semen analysis.
